# SWOT Tank Water Tracker for Sri Lanka

Oct 26, 2026

## Introduction

Sri Lankan farming runs on water stored in tanks and reservoirs, but storage figures are reported mainly for major reservoirs, not small village tanks. In 2018 FAO named the dry zone's cascaded tank-village system (ellanga gammana) a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System ([Daily FT](https://www.ft.lk/Agriculture/Sri-Lanka-designated-as-a-globally-important-agricultural-heritage-site/31-653552)). The Irrigation Department reports storage for its major reservoirs, such as 75% total capacity in December 2023 ([Daily FT](https://ft.lk/news/Total-water-capacity-of-irrigation-reservoirs-estimated-at-75/56-756207)).

This project builds an AI system that estimates how much water each tank holds, using free satellite data. It combines water area, mapped by deep learning from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2, with water height from SWOT, a NASA and French space agency (CNES) satellite launched in December 2022.

Farmers and irrigation officials can then see storage for every tank in a cascade and plan the next Yala or Maha crop.

## Problem Statement

Satellites already show where tank water is, but not how much water each tank holds.

- **Area is not volume.** IFAD's 2026 maps track the water surface of more than 10,650 water bodies in six districts at 10 m resolution, but not their volume ([IFAD](https://www.ifad.org/en/w/publications/water-surface-maps-to-manage-ancient-tank-cascade-systems-in-sri-lanka)). A shallow tank and a deep tank of the same area hold very different amounts of water.
- **Water height used to be out of reach.** Earlier altimeter satellites measured only along the line under their path; SWOT's wide swath covers far more ground ([AVISO](https://www.aviso.altimetry.fr/en/missions/current-missions/swot/orbit/science-orbit.html)) and is specified for lakes down to 250 m × 250 m ([NASA ARSET](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/arset-swot-part1-qa.pdf)).
- **Clouds and water plants hide the water.** Optical images fail under monsoon clouds, and floating plants cover water surfaces; in North Bolgoda Lake water hyacinth spread most from May to November ([Dawalagala et al., 2023](http://dl.lib.uom.lk/items/c7fd94f4-b934-44a7-87ac-63f4e30cd985)).
- **No tool joins these for Sri Lanka.** No study found combines SWOT water heights with AI water maps to give storage for Sri Lankan tanks.

## Research Questions and Objectives

The project tests whether free satellite data and AI can report water storage for small village tanks, not just the major reservoirs.

**Research questions**

1. How accurately can deep learning map tank water from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2, including tanks under cloud or partly covered by water plants?
2. How accurately does SWOT measure water height on major reservoirs and on village tanks close to its 6.25 ha size limit, compared with gauge readings, and can the readings it flags as "suspect" be trusted?
3. Does combining AI water area with SWOT height give storage close to official and measured values?
4. Can storage be forecast 2–4 weeks ahead from recent storage and rainfall? (stretch)

**Main objective:** build and validate a system that reports storage for five major reservoirs and eight village tanks near Anuradhapura, using only free satellite data.

**Specific objectives**

- Build a labelled dataset of Sri Lankan tank water masks, including cloudy and plant-covered cases.
- Train a deep-learning model that maps water through clouds and under floating plants.
- Process SWOT water heights for the study tanks and check them against gauge readings.
- Build area–height–volume curves per tank and estimate storage over time.
- Deliver a dashboard showing storage for each study tank.

## Literature Review and Research Gap

SWOT storage has been shown on large US reservoirs and, combined with other satellites' water area, on 245 reservoirs in five countries; SWOT heights have been checked on Taiwanese ponds, while Sri Lankan work maps water area, or water level from ICESat-2 only every 91 days. We found no study combining them for Sri Lankan tanks; the closest, a 2026 Thai abstract, maps water and floating plants with Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 but uses no SWOT.

**This project's contribution:** the first published SWOT storage study for Sri Lankan tanks, checked against local gauge readings, plus an AI water map that handles clouds and water plants and updates storage every 5–12 days. SWOT's own lake product already reports storage change for listed lakes; this project adds the AI maps, small tanks and ground checks.

## Study Area and Data Sources

The study covers 13 tanks east of Anuradhapura: five major reservoirs with daily gauges and eight village tanks within about 5 km of Mahakanadarawa. They were chosen after the October 2026 data check described in the Feasibility Check section below.

**Why these tanks**

- **Seen by SWOT:** all 13 lie east of the blind strip of SWOT pass 273, and passes 273 and 092 both cover them, so each is seen twice every 21 days.
- **Listed by SWOT:** all 13 have a lake ID in SWOT's Prior Lake Database, so their heights come straight from the Hydrocron API, with 47–90 usable readings each since July 2023.
- **Ground truth:** the five major reservoirs have daily Irrigation Department gauge readings. They give firm results and show how far SWOT's "suspect" readings can be trusted.
- **A range of village tanks:** 49 village tanks of 6.25–25 ha near the five reservoirs pass the SWOT checks, and Mahakanadarawa has the most nearby (19). The eight chosen lie 0.4–5.1 km from it: two with open water, three mixed and three mostly covered by plants. Tanks 1 and 2 are close to SWOT's 6.25 ha (250 m × 250 m) size limit, so they test it.
- **Still to confirm:** whether the eight village tanks share one cascade (on the Irrigation Department's cascade map), and their local names; most have no name in OpenStreetMap.

## Feasibility Check

Before fixing the study area, we tested whether the project's data can really be built. In October 2026 we pulled every SWOT lake reading from July 2023 to 11 October 2026 for a 44 × 55 km box east of Anuradhapura, and checked it against Irrigation Department gauges, Sentinel-2 images and open training data. **Verdict: feasible, with the five changes listed below the table.**

| Check | What we found | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Does SWOT list the tanks? | 402 SWOT lakes in the box. 58% of mapped tanks of 6.25 ha or more have a SWOT lake ID, from 34% at 6.25–10 ha to 100% above 100 ha; 86% of Irrigation Department tanks have one. All 13 study tanks have one. | Risk, handled by the choice of tanks |
| 2. Are the heights usable? | 26,641 readings on 400 lakes. The five major reservoirs have 53–71 best-quality readings each. Village tanks get almost only "suspect" readings, but 103 of 104 tanks of 6.25–25 ha have 10 or more usable readings, with a clear yearly rise and fall (median 2.3 m). | Risk, tested in RQ2 |
| 3. Do the heights match the gauges? | SWOT minus gauge: Hurulu Wewa −0.26 m, Mahakanadarawa +0.34 m, Nuwara Wewa +0.77 m, Nachchaduwa −1.14 m, with readings 13–20 days apart. No large datum error; same-day readings are needed for a true accuracy test. | OK as a rough check |
| 4. Can Sentinel-2 map the water? | Only 17–22% of Sentinel-2 dates were clear over the three test tanks (0–3 a month, fewest from November to February). Water area followed SWOT height at Nuwara Wewa (Spearman 0.70, only 5 pairs) but less well at two village tanks (0.68 after dropping one bad SWOT reading, and 0.22), where plants covered 49–81% of the surface. | Risk, so Sentinel-1 is now core |
| 5. Is there training data? | Sen1Floods11 (Bonafilia et al., 2020): 446 hand-labelled image tiles, 1.75 GB, free. S1S2-Water (Wieland et al., 2024): free, but 165 GB in seven parts. | Pass |

A best-quality reading has quality flag 0, crossover flag 1 or less and dark water 10% or less (Köhn and Nielsen, 2026); a "suspect" reading has quality flag 1.

**What changed in the plan**

- **Study area:** 13 tanks that SWOT lists and sees twice every 21 days, east of the blind strip of SWOT pass 273.
- **SWOT heights:** village tanks keep their "suspect" readings after outlier screening. RQ2 tests how far such readings can be trusted, using the five gauged reservoirs, which have both kinds. SWOT's own early validation found that suspect readings raise the error only from 12 to 14 cm on lakes of 250 m × 250 m to 1 km² (Fjørtoft and Pottier, 2025).
- **Water area:** Sentinel-1 radar and the "water under plants" class are core, not extras. If area still fails on plant-covered tanks, their curve comes from Imbulana's (2022) relative depth–area–capacity equation, which needs only four or five area readings.
- **Ground truth:** the Irrigation Department's Reservoir Status page shows only the current day, so we save its data automatically every day (since October 2026) and will request daily levels since July 2023.
- **Training data:** Sen1Floods11 plus one part of S1S2-Water (33 GB at most), not all 165 GB.

**Cost and tools.** All data and software are free, and the scripts used for this check already download and filter SWOT, Sentinel-2 and gauge data, so the pipeline starts from working code. The only cost is travel: every study tank is within about 40 km of Anuradhapura.

## Methodology

SWOT passes build each tank's height–area curve; after that, every new Sentinel water map converts straight into storage, so updates come every 5–12 days instead of every 21.

&#91;embedded content: method pipeline · two satellite inputs, one storage output\]

The left path runs every 5–12 days; the SWOT path only needs enough passes, from its record since July 2023, to fit each tank's curve.

1. **Tank inventory:** record each study tank's outline, local name, SWOT lake ID and the SWOT passes that cover it; the feasibility check has already found the IDs and passes.
2. **Labelled dataset:** create water masks for many dates per tank. Start from water indices on clear Sentinel-2 images, correct them by hand, and mark three classes: open water, water under plants, and land.
3. **AI water mapping:** train a segmentation network (for example U-Net) on Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 together, so it still works when clouds block Sentinel-2. The tank's recent history helps it spot water hidden under plants.
4. **SWOT heights:** pull each tank's full height record since July 2023 through the Hydrocron API. Keep best-quality readings; for village tanks, which SWOT flags as "suspect", keep those too after removing outliers. Tanks without a Prior Lake Database ID need the SWOT pixel cloud instead.
5. **Height–area–volume curves:** pair each SWOT height with the AI water area from the same date, fit a curve per tank, and integrate it to get volume. [Ma et al., 2026](https://impacts.ucar.edu/en/publications/monitoring-reservoir-storage-using-swot-satellite-observations-an/) used SWOT's simultaneous height and area the same way. SWOT only sees levels a tank has reached since 2023, so the curve gives storage above the lowest level seen; Irrigation Department capacity tables, or a fitted bowl shape, give total storage and % full.
6. **Storage time series:** convert every new water map into height and volume through the curve, and report storage change, plus % of full capacity where the capacity is known.
7. **Forecasting (stretch):** predict storage 2–4 weeks ahead from recent storage and rainfall.
8. **Dashboard:** a map of the study tanks with each tank's current storage, trend and last update.

## Evaluation Plan

Every result is checked against field or official data, and every AI model is compared with a simpler baseline.

| What is tested | Metric | Compared against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water mapping | IoU and F1 for water; reported separately for cloudy and plant-covered tanks | Water-index thresholds on Sentinel-2, a Sentinel-1 threshold method, and IFAD/ESA maps where shared |
| SWOT water height | Error in cm against gauge readings on pass dates, by tank size and for best-quality versus "suspect" readings, after matching each gauge's zero to SWOT (Sri Lankan gauges read about 0.4 m below ICESat-2; Madumalsha et al., 2025) | SWOT requirement (Fjørtoft and Pottier, 2025): 10 cm (1σ) for lakes over 1 km²; goal: 25 cm (1σ) for lakes from 250 m × 250 m to 1 km² ([NASA ARSET](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/arset-swot-part1-qa.pdf)) |
| Storage | Error as a percentage of capacity against Irrigation Department figures and gauge-based volumes | Area-only estimate that assumes a fixed depth |
| Update frequency | Days between storage updates | SWOT alone (21-day repeat) |
| Forecast (stretch) | Error at 2 and 4 weeks ahead | "No change" baseline |
| Usefulness | Feedback from irrigation officers and farmer organisations | — |

## Expected Outcomes and Contributions

The project delivers the first published, gauge-checked SWOT storage figures for Sri Lankan tanks, plus the data and tools to repeat them.

1. **Evidence:** SWOT's accuracy on Sri Lankan tanks, measured against gauges and broken down by tank size.
2. **Model and dataset:** an AI water map that works under clouds and floating plants, with the labelled Sri Lankan tank dataset behind it.
3. **Storage curves:** a height–area–volume curve for every studied tank, reusable by irrigation engineers.
4. **Tool:** a dashboard showing storage for the 13 study tanks, updated every 5–12 days.
5. **Publication:** a paper for a conference such as UoM's [ICITR](https://icitr.uom.lk/abstracts/abstract-2024.pdf).

## Scope

The core is deliberately limited to 13 tanks near Anuradhapura, because the full idea is larger than a typical final-year project. UoM IT final-year computer vision papers usually fine-tune a pretrained model on about 600–1,250 images and add a web app ([ICITR 2024](https://icitr.uom.lk/abstracts/abstract-2024.pdf)), while most UoM satellite studies use one satellite and classic classifiers. This project sits above the first and close to Master's-level satellite work.

| Level | Included | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core (must deliver) | Five major reservoirs and eight village tanks; AI water mapping from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 with a water-plant class; SWOT heights, including screened "suspect" readings for village tanks; height–area–volume curves; storage time series; validation; simple dashboard | Answers research questions 1–3 end to end; the feasibility check showed that Sentinel-1 and the plant class are needed, not extras |
| Stretch (if time allows) | Storage forecast 2–4 weeks ahead; SWOT pixel cloud for tanks missing from the lake product; cloud-removal module; more of the 49 village tanks that passed the SWOT checks | Adds research question 4 and wider coverage |
| Out of scope | Whole-island coverage; mobile app; real-time alerts; surveys of tank beds | Too large for 3 people in 8 months |

## Risks, Ethics and Safety

The biggest risk, SWOT not seeing the tanks, was checked in October 2026 (see Feasibility Check): SWOT sees all 13 study tanks twice every 21 days. The main risks now are "suspect" readings on village tanks, and water hidden by plants and clouds.

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| SWOT passes rarely, or not at all, over the chosen tanks | Checked: passes 273 and 092 both cover all 13 tanks, with 47–90 usable readings per tank since July 2023 |
| Small tanks missing from SWOT's lake product | Checked: all 13 study tanks have a lake ID, though only 58% of mapped tanks of 6.25 ha or more do; adding tanks without one needs the pixel cloud (stretch) |
| SWOT flags almost all village-tank readings as "suspect", and a few are metres off | Remove outliers; use the five gauged reservoirs, which have both kinds of reading, to measure how good "suspect" readings are (RQ2); report village tanks separately |
| A chosen village tank turns out unsuitable (another cascade, no access or no gauge post) | 41 other village tanks pass the same SWOT checks; swap one in |
| No access to official gauge records | Save the Irrigation Department's Reservoir Status data every day (automated since October 2026); request daily levels since July 2023 through the supervisor in the first month; photograph gauge posts on pass dates |
| Floating plants hide water: three of the eight village tanks are mostly plant-covered | Separate water-plant class plus the tank's history; report accuracy for these tanks separately; Sentinel-1 alone can mistake dense plant cover for land, so clear Sentinel-2 dates anchor these tanks; fallback: SWOT height with Imbulana's (2022) depth–area–capacity equation |
| Monsoon clouds | Sentinel-1 radar as a core input; Sentinel-2 used when clear (only 17–22% of dates were clear over the test tanks) |
| Field safety | Visit in daylight and in pairs, read gauges from the bund, never enter the water; dry-zone tanks can have elephants and crocodiles nearby |
| Permission | Inform the irrigation office and farmer organisation before any visit |
| Data volume | Process in Google Earth Engine and through the Hydrocron API; download only the study area; use Sen1Floods11 and one part of S1S2-Water, not all 165 GB |
