What Sentinel sees from above

When the water drops, the edge moves in. The dashed line shows the tank when it’s full. Floating plants can hide part of the water: a plain water map then shows too small an area, and too little water. Our AI model has a “water under plants” class for this.

The curve that links them

Each faint dot is a past day when SWOT and Sentinel both saw the tank. There are no dots below 100 m, so that part of the curve is only a guess.

A year of updates

  • Sentinel-1 radar, about every 12 days (sometimes 6), sees through cloud
  • Sentinel-2 photo, every 5 days, but only about 1 in 5 is clear
  • SWOT pass, about twice every 21 days; on a tank this small it is typically off by about 13 cm
  • Real water, for comparison

Made-up numbers for a made-up tank: the water area grows by 10 hectares for every metre of height, the bed is at 99 m, and the tank spills over at 102 m. In our project, the heights come from SWOT’s Hydrocron data and the areas come from our AI water maps. The timing and errors follow real data: from October 2025 to October 2026, Sentinel-1 passed over Hurulu Wewa on 35 dates, mostly 12 days apart; about 1 in 5 Sentinel-2 photos of our test tanks was clear; SWOT passes our tanks about twice every 21 days, and on lakes this small its heights scatter by about 12–14 cm (SWOT lake validation, 2025).
